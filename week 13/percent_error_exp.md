## Significance of this percent error

The theoretical end effector position is the x, y, and z end effectors gotten from the q1 and q2 angles of the two jointed robotic arm and the base rotation that rotates the world frame by the z axis, but the rotation does not affect the y-axis much. To find the theoretical end effector position, you have to find the local frame x and z position to find the correct inverse kinematics angles from the recieved enf effector x, y, z positions. You do this by applying a rotation transform with the world x and z positions and the current base rotation. This is because the q1, q2 first gotten from the inverse kinematics calculator gives the world frame angles from the 0 origin of the robotic arm joints, since the x and z positions that the IK takes are bound to the entire frame of the robot. Afterwards, these q1 and q2 are passed on to the 2D forward kinematics calculator, first assuming that the robotic arm is 2D and it moves in the xz axis. Afterwards, a rotation transform is performed with respect to the angle of the base rotation to get the actual x, y, and z position of the end effector given the direction of the robotic arm in the 3D forward kinematics calculator. These end effector positions are compared to the original experimental end effector positions by an absolute percent error method. The percent error is not exactly 0, but really close to 0, because of the precision of the record end effector values (4 digits after the decimal) compared to the 7 or more digits after the decimal for the inverse and forward kinematics calculations. The main cause of error is the fact that for the fk and ik calculations, we are assuming that l1=l2=108.7, but if we ccheck the actual link length in unity, there might be a difference of around 2-5 cm, it's just that those l1 and l2 values better explained the ik of the robot. Finally, there is the y end effector value estimation, where for simplicity, since the y position only fluctuates by around 2 cm, we are assuming it to be constantly 0 for the calculations, which is why the percent error for y is around 1.1% compared to the other end effector position percent errors, which are around 0.2%.
